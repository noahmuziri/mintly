# Mintly lesson video scripts

One script per lesson, about 60–90 seconds each when read aloud. Each scene is one slide in `slides.html`, so you can screen-record the slides while reading the narration.

Shot list: record the slide full screen, read the narration, add the on-screen text as captions.


## What is financial literacy?

**Scene 1: Money decisions are everywhere**
- On screen: Snacks, games, phones, first jobs · Every choice has a trade-off
- Narration: "You make money decisions every single day, usually without noticing. Financial literacy is simply knowing enough to make those choices on purpose."

**Scene 2: It's a skill, not a talent**
- On screen: Nobody is born knowing this · Skills get better with practice
- Narration: "Nobody is born good with money. It's a skill, which means anyone can learn it, and the earlier you start, the more it helps."

**Scene 3: The four big ideas**
- On screen: Earn · Spend · Save · Invest
- Narration: "Everything in this course sits on four ideas: earning money, spending it wisely, saving some, and making it grow."

**Scene 4: Trade-offs and opportunity cost**
- On screen: Spending on one thing means not spending on another · The thing you give up is the real cost
- Narration: "Every dollar can only be used once. What you give up when you choose something is called the opportunity cost."

**Scene 5: Your turn**
- On screen: Do the warm-up · Finish the quiz · Move to lesson 2
- Narration: "Do the warm-up, take the quick quiz, and then let's build your first budget."


## Needs vs. wants: building a simple budget

**Scene 1: Needs vs. wants**
- On screen: Needs: you can't do without · Wants: nice to have
- Narration: "A need is something you must have to stay safe, healthy and get to school or work. A want is everything else, however much you love it."

**Scene 2: The gray area**
- On screen: A phone is a need. The newest one is a want · Ask: what's the basic version?
- Narration: "Some things are both. You may need a phone, but you probably don't need the newest model. Ask what the basic version would cost."

**Scene 3: A budget is a plan**
- On screen: Income in · Spending out · Savings on purpose
- Narration: "A budget isn't a punishment. It's a plan that tells your money where to go before it disappears."

**Scene 4: Build one in four steps**
- On screen: List your income · List your needs · Set a wants limit · Pay yourself first
- Narration: "List what comes in, cover your needs, set a limit for wants, and move something to savings first."

**Scene 5: Check in**
- On screen: Review every month · Adjust, don't quit
- Narration: "A budget gets better each month. If it doesn't fit, adjust it. Don't throw it away."


## Saving for something you want

**Scene 1: Pick a goal**
- On screen: Make it specific · Give it a price and a date
- Narration: "Vague goals don't get funded. Name the thing, find its price and pick a date."

**Scene 2: Do the math**
- On screen: Price ÷ weeks = weekly amount · $120 in 12 weeks is $10 a week
- Narration: "Divide the price by the number of weeks. A $120 goal in twelve weeks is just ten dollars a week."

**Scene 3: Make it automatic**
- On screen: Pay yourself first · Move money on payday
- Narration: "Move the money the day you get it. What you don't see, you don't spend."

**Scene 4: Emergency fund**
- On screen: Small cushion for surprises · Start with $100
- Narration: "Surprises happen. A small emergency fund keeps one bad day from becoming a debt."

**Scene 5: Stay motivated**
- On screen: Track it · Celebrate milestones
- Narration: "Track your progress where you can see it, and celebrate the halfway point."


## How banks work: accounts, debit cards and interest

**Scene 1: What a bank does**
- On screen: Keeps money safe · Lends money out · Pays you interest on savings
- Narration: "A bank keeps your money safe, and lends some of what everyone deposits to other people, paying you a little interest for the use of yours."

**Scene 2: Checking vs. savings**
- On screen: Checking: spending · Savings: growing
- Narration: "Checking accounts are for everyday spending. Savings accounts are for money you want to keep and grow."

**Scene 3: Debit cards**
- On screen: Spend money you already have · Watch for fees and overdrafts
- Narration: "A debit card spends money you already have. It's not credit, and some accounts charge fees, so read the terms."

**Scene 4: Interest**
- On screen: APY is what you earn in a year · Higher is better for savers
- Narration: "Interest is the price of using money. When you save, the bank pays you. Compare the APY to find the best deal."

**Scene 5: Keep it safe**
- On screen: FDIC insured in the US · Never share your PIN
- Narration: "In the US, deposits at insured banks are protected up to the limit. Never share your PIN or passwords."


## Intro to investing: stocks, risk and diversification

**Scene 1: Saving vs. investing**
- On screen: Saving is safe and small · Investing can grow more and can lose
- Narration: "Saving keeps money safe. Investing aims to grow it more, but it comes with the chance of losing some."

**Scene 2: What a stock is**
- On screen: A small piece of a company · Price moves with demand
- Narration: "A stock is a tiny piece of ownership in a company. Its price moves as people's opinions of that company change."

**Scene 3: Risk and return**
- On screen: Higher possible reward means higher risk · No guarantees
- Narration: "Higher possible returns almost always come with higher risk. No one can promise you a profit."

**Scene 4: Diversify**
- On screen: Don't put everything in one place · Funds hold many companies
- Narration: "Spreading your money across many things means one bad pick can't sink you. Index funds do this for you."

**Scene 5: Think long-term**
- On screen: Time smooths the bumps · Avoid hype
- Narration: "Prices bounce around day to day. Long time horizons and calm decisions beat chasing hype."


## Earning money: jobs, paychecks and taxes

**Scene 1: Ways teens earn**
- On screen: Allowance and chores · Informal work · Part-time jobs · Making and selling
- Narration: "Teens earn money in many ways, from chores and babysitting to first part-time jobs."

**Scene 2: Gross vs. net**
- On screen: Gross: before deductions · Net: what you take home
- Narration: "Gross pay is what you earn. Net pay is what lands in your account after taxes and deductions."

**Scene 3: Where it goes**
- On screen: Social Security 6.2% · Medicare 1.45% · Income tax withholding
- Narration: "Payroll taxes take 7.65 percent. Income tax withholding depends on your W-4, and low earners often owe little or none."

**Scene 4: Read your pay stub**
- On screen: Hours × rate = gross · Check the deductions · Check the net
- Narration: "Check hours, rate and deductions. If something looks wrong, ask your employer."

**Scene 5: Budget with net**
- On screen: Plan with take-home · Not the headline number
- Narration: "Always budget with your net pay. It's the only number you can actually spend."


## Credit and debt: borrowing without getting stuck

**Scene 1: Borrowing costs money**
- On screen: Interest is the price · APR is the yearly rate
- Narration: "When you borrow, you pay extra, called interest. APR is the yearly rate."

**Scene 2: The minimum-payment trap**
- On screen: $1,000 at 24% APR, $25 a month · About 7 years, roughly double the cost
- Narration: "Paying only the minimum can take years and cost nearly double the purchase price."

**Scene 3: Good debt, costly debt**
- On screen: Some debt builds your future · High-interest debt drains it
- Narration: "Some borrowing can help you build a future. Credit card and payday debt usually just costs."

**Scene 4: Your credit score**
- On screen: Pay on time · Keep balances low · Don't apply for everything
- Narration: "A credit score shows lenders how reliably you repay. On-time payments matter most."

**Scene 5: Buy now, pay later**
- On screen: Still debt · Late fees add up
- Narration: "Buy-now-pay-later splits a price into pieces, but it's still debt. Don't let small payments hide a big total."


## Time, inflation and compounding

**Scene 1: Inflation**
- On screen: Prices rise over time · Money buys less
- Narration: "Prices tend to rise over time, so the same dollar buys less in the future."

**Scene 2: Compound interest**
- On screen: Interest on your interest · Growth speeds up
- Narration: "Compounding means you earn interest on your interest, so growth speeds up the longer it runs."

**Scene 3: The Rule of 72**
- On screen: 72 ÷ rate = years to double · At 6%, about 12 years
- Narration: "Divide 72 by the rate to guess how many years it takes your money to double."

**Scene 4: Start early**
- On screen: $50 a month from 16 vs. 26 · Waiting costs tens of thousands
- Narration: "Starting ten years earlier can mean tens of thousands more, from the same monthly amount."

**Scene 5: What to do**
- On screen: Save a little now · Keep it growing
- Narration: "You don't need much. You need time. Start with whatever you can."


## Scams and staying safe with money

**Scene 1: Scams teens meet**
- On screen: Fake delivery texts · Overpayment · Money mule jobs · Pump-and-dump
- Narration: "Scammers target teens with fake packages, too-good jobs and hyped-up stocks."

**Scene 2: Red flags**
- On screen: Pressure and urgency · Unusual payment methods · Too good to be true · Secret or private
- Narration: "Pressure, odd payment methods and secrecy are the biggest warning signs."

**Scene 3: Pause, verify, tell**
- On screen: Slow down · Check the real source · Tell a trusted adult
- Narration: "When something feels off: pause, verify with the real company, and tell someone you trust."

**Scene 4: Protect your accounts**
- On screen: Long unique passwords · Two-factor sign-in · Never share one-time codes
- Narration: "Use strong passwords, switch on two-factor, and never share a one-time code with anyone."

**Scene 5: If it happens**
- On screen: It isn't your fault · Tell someone quickly · Contact your bank
- Narration: "If you get caught, it isn't your fault. Tell a trusted adult and contact your bank fast."


## Your money plan: putting it all together

**Scene 1: The plan**
- On screen: Income · Needs · Wants · Savings and goals
- Narration: "Your plan brings the whole course together in one page."

**Scene 2: 50/30/20**
- On screen: 50% needs · 30% wants · 20% saving
- Narration: "A simple starting split is 50 percent needs, 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings."

**Scene 3: Make it automatic**
- On screen: Pay yourself first · Set up transfers
- Narration: "Automate what you can so good habits don't depend on willpower."

**Scene 4: Check in monthly**
- On screen: What worked? · What changes?
- Narration: "Review once a month. Small adjustments keep your plan realistic."

**Scene 5: Next: practice**
- On screen: Run the simulation · Practice with play money
- Narration: "Now put it to work. Try the simulation, with play money, and see how you decide."
