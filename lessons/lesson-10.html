Financial Literacy · Lesson 10

Your money plan: putting it all together

About 15 minutes · 5 activities · Your answers and progress are saved to your account

You've now seen the whole picture: earning, budgeting, saving, banking, investing, borrowing, compounding and staying safe. This last lesson ties them into one plan that fits your life. It doesn't need to be perfect; it needs to be yours.

Warm-up · Typing

What's one money goal for the next year?

It could be saving for something, earning a certain amount, or learning one new skill with money. Write it down.

The plan, step by step

  1. Know what comes in. Use your net (take-home) pay and any allowance or gifts (Lessons 1 and 6).
  2. Split it on purpose. A simple starting point is the 50/30/20 rule: about 50% for needs, 30% for wants, 20% for savings and goals. Adjust it to your life (Lesson 2).
  3. Name a goal. A goal with an amount and a date is far easier to reach than "save more" (Lesson 3).
  4. Build a cushion. An emergency fund covers surprises so you don't need to borrow (Lesson 3).
  5. Keep it in the right place. A savings account that is insured and pays interest (Lesson 4).
  6. Avoid costly debt. Pay cards in full, read the APR, and be wary of "easy payments" (Lesson 7).
  7. Let time work. Invest for the long term, spread across many investments, and start early (Lessons 5 and 8).
  8. Stay safe. Pause, verify, tell, and use 2FA (Lesson 9).

Make it automatic

The best plans don't rely on willpower. Pay yourself first: move your savings share the day you're paid, before you can spend it. If your bank offers it, set up an automatic transfer.

Check in and adjust

Look at your plan once a month. Did you hit your savings amount? Did your income or goals change? A plan you adjust is a plan that lasts.
