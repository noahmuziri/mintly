Side hustles: revenue, costs and profit

About 14 minutes · a profit lab · Your answers are saved in this browser

Quick story

You mow five lawns at $20 each and tell your friends you made $100. Then you remember the gas, the new trimmer line, and the app fee. How much did you actually keep? That gap between what comes in and what you keep is the most important number in any business.

A side hustle is any way of earning that's not a regular job: mowing lawns, tutoring, reselling, making things to sell, editing videos, walking dogs. It's one of the best ways to learn about money, because everything is real and the stakes are small.

Revenue is not profit

  • Revenue is all the money that comes in.
  • Costs are everything you spend to earn it. Variable costs change with each sale (supplies, gas). Fixed costs stay the same (an app subscription, equipment you bought once).
  • Profit is revenue minus costs. It's what you actually keep.

How to set a price

Start with what it costs you to do the job, add a fair amount for your time, and then check what others charge. Too low and you're working for free. Too high and nobody buys. Ask: what is an hour of my time worth, and what would the customer pay for this result?

The grown-up stuff

  • Keep records. Write down what you earn and spend. It makes profit obvious and makes taxes easy.
  • Taxes. In the US, if you earn $400 or more in a year from self-employment you generally need to file a return and may owe self-employment tax. Rules change, so check current guidance or ask a parent.
  • Stay safe. Work with a parent or guardian involved, don't share your home address with strangers online, and check a platform's age rules before you sign up.
  • Reinvest a little. Putting some profit back into better supplies or marketing is how a hustle grows.

Key words from this lesson

Revenue
All the money coming in.
Variable cost
A cost that changes with each sale.
Fixed cost
A cost that stays the same every month.
Profit
Revenue minus costs. What you keep.
Break-even
The point where revenue equals costs.
Margin
Profit as a share of the price.
