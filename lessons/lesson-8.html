Paychecks and taxes: why take-home is smaller

About 12 minutes · 2 activities · Your answers are saved in this browser

Your first paycheck is exciting, and then you open it. You worked 15 hours at $15 an hour, so you were expecting $225, but the amount that landed is smaller. Where did the rest go?

This lesson explains what's taken out of a paycheck and why, what the paperwork for a first job means, and how to read a pay stub. The examples use the United States. Other countries have different rules, but the ideas are similar.

Gross pay and net pay

  • Gross pay is what you earned before anything is taken out: hours worked times your hourly rate.
  • Net pay (take-home pay) is what you actually receive after deductions.

What gets taken out

  • Social Security and Medicare (FICA). Together these take 7.65% of most paychecks. They fund retirement, disability and healthcare programs, and they count toward benefits you'll use later.
  • Federal income tax. Based on how much you earn in a year. Many teens earn little enough that they owe no federal income tax at all.
  • State and local taxes. These depend on where you live, and some places have none.
Good to know Employers sometimes withhold income tax from a teen's paycheck even when the teen won't owe any. If you earn less than the standard deduction for the year, you can file a tax return to get that withheld money back. Ask a parent or guardian to help the first time.

Your first-job paperwork

  • Form W-4 tells your employer how much income tax to withhold. If you're not sure, ask a parent or the employer to explain each line.
  • Form I-9 proves you're allowed to work, usually with ID such as a passport or a combination of documents.
  • Pay stub comes with each paycheck and lists hours, gross pay, every deduction and net pay. Check it every time. Mistakes happen.
  • Form W-2 arrives in January and summarizes the year. You'll use it to file a tax return.

Other kinds of income

Not all income comes from an employer. Babysitting, lawn-mowing or selling things you make is called self-employment. It's real income, and over a certain amount you may need to report it and pay self-employment tax. Keep a simple record of what you earn and spend on it, so tax time isn't a surprise.

Quick example Priya works 15 hours at $15 an hour: $225 gross. FICA takes 7.65%, about $17. If no income tax is withheld, her take-home is about $208. She keeps her pay stub, and at tax time she files to make sure she didn't pay anything she didn't owe.
