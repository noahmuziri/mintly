Insurance: paying a little to dodge a big loss

About 12 minutes · a calculator · Your answers are saved in this browser

Quick story

A new phone costs $800. The protection plan is $9 a month. If you drop it, they fix it for a $50 fee. Do you pay the $9 every month? How would you decide? That decision is the whole idea of insurance.

Insurance is a deal: you pay a small amount regularly, and in return the insurance company covers a big loss if it happens. Lots of people pay in, a few have a loss, and the money from everyone covers those few. It spreads risk around.

The words you'll hear

  • Premium: what you pay (monthly or yearly) to have the coverage.
  • Deductible: what you pay first before insurance pays the rest.
  • Claim: a request for the insurance company to pay for a covered loss.
  • Coverage: what the policy will and won't pay for. Always read what's excluded.

Common kinds

  • Health: protects you from huge medical bills.
  • Auto: required by law almost everywhere if you drive.
  • Renters or home: covers your stuff and some accidents.
  • Phone and gadgets: optional, and often poor value.

When insurance is worth it

The best insurance is for losses that are big, unlikely, and too expensive to cover yourself: a hospital stay, a car accident, a house fire. It's usually a poor deal for small losses you could afford, like a $60 pair of headphones.

Smart habits

  • Insure the catastrophes, and save for the small stuff (your emergency fund from Lesson 3 does this job).
  • A higher deductible usually means a lower premium. Pick one you could actually pay.
  • Compare quotes. The same coverage can cost very different amounts.
  • Read the exclusions. "Covered" doesn't mean covered for everything.
