Financial Literacy · Lesson 14

Fact or hype: reading money news without getting played

About 12 minutes · a game · Your answers are saved in this browser

Quick story

A video pops up: “I turned $500 into $50,000 in one week. Link in bio.” It has a million views. What's the first thing you check? Hint: it isn't the number.

Money advice is everywhere: videos, group chats, comment sections, and creators with fancy cars. Some of it is useful. A lot of it is built to make you feel something so you'll act fast. This lesson is about slowing down and checking.

How hype works on you

  • FOMO: "Everyone's getting in, you'll miss out." Fear of missing out makes you rush.
  • Scarcity and deadlines: "Only 3 spots left." "Ends tonight."
  • Social proof: screenshots, big numbers, lots of likes. All easy to fake or cherry-pick.
  • Authority: a confident voice, a nice car, a suit. None of that is evidence.
  • Survivorship bias: you see the one person who won, not the hundreds who lost.

The incentive check

Before you trust any money advice, ask: how does this person get paid? Affiliate links, sponsored posts, selling a course, or owning the thing they're pushing all change what they say. "Pump and dump" schemes work by hyping something so the people who already own it can sell to you at the top.

A five-question filter

  • Who is saying this, and what do they gain?
  • Where's the evidence? A real source, not a screenshot.
  • What's the risk? If nobody mentions it, that's a red flag.
  • What's the time frame? "Fast" and "guaranteed" don't go together with investing.
  • What would a skeptic say? Go find them.
