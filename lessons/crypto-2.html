Cryptocurrencies · Lesson 2

Volatility and the maths of risk

About 13 minutes · 5 activities · Your answers and progress are saved to your account

Crypto prices can jump 20% in a day, and fall just as fast. This lesson shows the maths that makes big drops so painful, the kinds of scams that follow the hype, and why the sentence "only use money you can afford to lose" actually matters.

Volatility

Volatility (noun)

How quickly and how far a price moves up and down. High volatility means big swings in both directions, which means big possible gains and big possible losses.

Stocks move, but most crypto moves far more. A single day of news, a tweet or a rumor can change prices a lot.

The surprising maths of losses

Losses are harder to undo than they look. A 50% fall needs a 100% rise to get back. A 90% fall needs a 900% rise. The bigger the drop, the less likely you ever fully recover.

Scams love hype

  • Rug pull: creators hype a new coin, collect money, then disappear and the price collapses to zero.
  • Giveaway scams: "send 1 coin and get 2 back". Nobody gives free money.
  • Impersonation: fake accounts of celebrities or support staff asking for your keys or recovery words.
  • Romance and "advisor" scams: a stranger online teaches you to invest on a fake site. You can never withdraw.
  • Pump-and-dump: a group hypes a coin, sells to everyone who joins late, and the price crashes.

Crypto transfers are usually irreversible. If you're tricked, there is rarely a way to get the money back.

"Only use money you can afford to lose"

This is not a slogan. If a coin could fall 80% in a month, then the money you put in should be money you wouldn't need next month, and that wouldn't ruin you if it vanished. Money for school, rent or emergencies does not belong anywhere near it.
