Collectables · Lesson 2

Condition, grading and fakes

About 11 minutes · 4 activities · Your answers and progress are saved to your account

Two items can look identical and sell for completely different prices. One has a tiny corner crease. The other might not be real at all. This lesson shows how condition, grading and authenticity change the price, and how to avoid getting fooled.

Warm-up · Typing

Would you pay full price for a damaged one?

You find two copies of something you want. One is perfect and costs $100. The other has visible wear and costs $60. Which would you buy, and why?

Condition matters a lot

Collectors look closely at wear: creases, scratches, fading, yellowing, missing parts. Items still in their original packaging, never opened or used, are often called mint or sealed and usually sell for the most. Small flaws can cut the price a lot.

What grading is

Grading (noun)

Paying an independent company to inspect an item, give it a numbered condition score and seal it in a protective case. A high grade can raise the price, but grading costs money and takes time, so it only makes sense for items that could be worth much more.

A grade is an opinion about condition, not a promise that the price will go up. Prices for graded items still move with demand like everything else.

Fakes and how to protect yourself

The more valuable an item gets, the more likely someone is faking it. Warning signs include:

  • A price far below what the same item usually sells for.
  • Blurry, copied or very few photos, or photos that don't show the details you ask about.
  • A seller with no history or reviews, who avoids questions or wants you to move to private messages.
  • Details that don't match the real thing, like the wrong font, colors, stitching or packaging.

Protect yourself by buying from sellers with a long record of good reviews, using payment methods with buyer protection, and asking an experienced collector or a parent to look before you pay for anything expensive.

Check what things actually sold for

The price a seller asks is a wish. The price something sold for is a fact. Before buying, look at completed sales of the same item in the same condition, not at the listings that haven't sold.

Key words from this lesson

Mint
Perfect or near-perfect condition.
Grading
Paid inspection that gives an item a condition score.
Authentic
Genuine, not a fake.
Counterfeit
A fake made to look real.
Sold price
What an item actually sold for.
Asking price
What a seller hopes to get.
