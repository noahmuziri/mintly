Cryptocurrencies · Lesson 1

What crypto actually is

About 11 minutes · 4 activities · Your answers and progress are saved to your account

You've probably heard crypto described as the future of money, or as a scam. The truth is more interesting, and less dramatic. This lesson explains what cryptocurrency is, how it works in plain language, and what gives it any value at all.

Warm-up · Typing

What do you think crypto is?

Write down what you already know or have heard about crypto, even if you're not sure it's right. Then we'll check it.

Digital money with no bank in the middle

Cryptocurrency (noun)

A digital token that people can send to each other over the internet, recorded on a shared public record instead of in a bank's private books.

With normal money, a bank keeps track of who owns what. With crypto, thousands of computers around the world keep copies of the same record and agree on every change. That shared record is called a blockchain.

Coins you may have heard of

  • Bitcoin: the first and best known. Its supply is capped at 21 million coins.
  • Ethereum: a network that also runs small programs, called smart contracts.
  • Stablecoins: tokens designed to hold the value of a regular currency like the dollar. They aim to be steadier, but they can still fail.
  • Thousands of others: many are created in minutes by anyone, and many end up worth nothing.

Wallets and keys

Crypto is held in a wallet, which is protected by a secret private key (often a list of recovery words). Whoever has the key controls the coins. If you lose it, the coins can be gone forever, and if someone steals it, there is usually no bank to call.

Where does the value come from?

A dollar is backed by a government and accepted for taxes. A company share is a slice of a business that earns profits. Most crypto has neither. Its price comes mainly from what people are willing to pay, which depends on belief and demand. That is why prices can swing so widely.
